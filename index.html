Playbook

The strategy behind the plan. Food-first, gradual, no supplements.

Timing is everything

  • Breakfast BEFORE meds — he'll actually be hungry. Biggest calorie window of the day. Meds are taken after breakfast: ~7:30am on weekdays, ~8:30am on weekends.
  • School lunch — he eats the school lunch. Only basic snacks can go to school.
  • After-school grazing — meds are still active, so no big plates. Graze-able snacks he can eat without sitting down.
  • Dinner AFTER meds wear off — evening rebound hunger. Second big window; serve the heaviest thing he'll eat.
  • Eat on a schedule — don't wait for hunger cues. If you wait for him to say he's hungry, midday won't happen.

Calorie boosters (add to anything)

  • Butter or olive oil in cooking
  • Whole milk instead of water (oatmeal, cream of wheat, pancakes)
  • Cheese on everything
  • Honey or maple drizzle
  • Extra mayo

Anytime smoothie (~500–700 cal)

Whole milk + banana + cocoa + honey + a scoop of oats, blended. To keep it in the 500–700 range without peanut butter, use a generous half-cup of oats plus an extra drizzle of honey, or a splash of cream. Easy to get down when he's not hungry.

His words

When asked directly, Jasper named his favorites: chicken nuggets, eggs, and fruit. The pools weight these heaviest.

He also likes spaghetti plain with butter (no cheese on top), and does not eat peanut butter — it never appears anywhere here.

Pediatrician

Since this involves his growth and his medication, keep his pediatrician in the loop — they'll want to monitor the trend and may have input on the medication side. Goal: 40th back to 50th percentile, gradual and food-first.
